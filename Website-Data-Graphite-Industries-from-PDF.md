Proposed Mine-to-Manufacturing Graphite Value Chain

## 01. Run-of-Mine (ROM) Graphite Ore

From Run-of-Mine Graphite to Value-Added Manufacturing Developing an integrated graphite processing and manufacturing platform in Mpwapwa District, Dodoma, Tanzania, serving regional and international market

The proposed value chain begins with the sourcing of run-of-mine (ROM) graphite-bearing ore from a suitable and verified mineral source. The development strategy is to establish a reliable feedstock supply supported by geological assessment, representative sampling, mineralogical characterisation and appropriate verification of mineral rights and source suitability.

Before processing requirements are finalised, the ore will need to be evaluated for graphite content, mineral associations, flake-size distribution, impurities, weathering characteristics and variability across

the deposit. These factors will influence the selection of mining methods, ore handling systems and downstream processing technologies.

The objective is to establish a dependable raw-material foundation for an integrated graphite operation, with feedstock quality and consistency aligned with the requirements of the proposed processing and manufacturing facilities.

## 02. Crushing and Grinding

Crushing and grinding will be evaluated as the initial mechanical processing stages for preparing graphite-bearing ore for mineral separation. The objective is to reduce the ore to suitable particle sizes while liberating graphite flakes from the surrounding gangue minerals.

The proposed approach will emphasise controlled size reduction because excessive grinding can damage larger graphite flakes and reduce the potential value of products that depend on flake size. The crushing and grinding circuit will therefore need to balance liberation efficiency, preservation of valuable graphite characteristics, energy consumption and processing performance.

The final equipment configuration, grinding stages and operating parameters will be determined through ore characterisation and metallurgical testwork. This will help establish a technically appropriate and resource-efficient preparation circuit for the selected feedstock.

## 03. Beneficiation and Flotation

Beneficiation will be assessed as the principal mineral-separation stage for concentrating graphite and reducing the proportion of unwanted minerals in the processed material. Depending on the ore's characteristics, the proposed circuit may incorporate flotation, supported by suitable conditioning, reagent selection and separation stages.

The purpose of flotation is to exploit differences in the surface properties of graphite and associated minerals to produce a graphite concentrate suitable for further upgrading. Process development will consider concentrate grade, graphite recovery, flake preservation, reagent consumption, water requirements and the management of tailings.

The appropriate flowsheet will depend on laboratory and, where justified, pilot-scale test results. No specific concentrate grade or recovery rate should be assumed before the ore has been evaluated and the proposed process has been demonstrated to perform satisfactorily.

## 04. Graphite Concentrate Purification

Following beneficiation, graphite concentrate may require additional purification to meet the quality specifications of its intended industrial applications. The purification strategy will be evaluated against the required purity, residual impurity profile, flake characteristics, environmental considerations and cost of processing.

Potential approaches may include further physical separation, thermal treatment or chemical purification, subject to technical evaluation and applicable environmental and safety requirements. The suitability of each method will depend on the mineralogical composition of the concentrate and the purity specifications sought by prospective customers.

Particular attention will be given to process efficiency, energy and water consumption, reagent management, waste treatment and the potential effects of purification on the graphite's physical properties. The objective is to identify a technically viable and commercially appropriate route for producing graphite materials that meet verified customer requirements.

The longer-term development objective is to evaluate opportunities to move beyond the sale of raw ore or unprocessed concentrate towards higher-value graphite products. ## 4. OPERATIONS AND PROJECT DEVELOPMENT

The Project

GRAPHITE INDUSTRIES LIMITED is evaluating a proposed graphite mining and processing project in Mpwapwa District, Dodoma Region, Tanzania.

The project concept is intended to investigate the technical and commercial requirements for processing graphite-bearing run-of-mine ore and producing upgraded graphite products.

The current planning basis includes a proposed processing capacity of 2,000 tonnes of ROM ore per day, with an operating schedule of three eight-hour shifts per day and 330 operating days annually.

These parameters are preliminary planning assumptions. They do not establish a completed feasibility study, confirmed production rate, product recovery, saleable output or commercial operating status.

Project Development Approach

Stage 1: Mineralogical Characterisation

Representative samples will need to be characterised to understand the mineral composition, graphite occurrence, liberation characteristics and relevant impurities.

The results will inform decisions on testing requirements and potential processing routes.

Stage 2: Metallurgical Testwork

Laboratory and, where appropriate, pilot-scale testwork will be used to investigate how the ore responds to candidate beneficiation methods.

Testing should establish evidence on concentrate quality, recovery, reagent requirements, grind characteristics and other parameters relevant to process selection.

Stage 3: Process Development

Subject to test results, suitable process flowsheets and equipment configurations can be developed for further technical evaluation.

Potential unit operations may include crushing, grinding, flotation, concentrate cleaning, dewatering, drying, classification and product- quality control. The final route will depend on the ore and the required product specifications.

Stage 4: Site and Infrastructure Assessment

The project will require appropriate evaluation of land status, topography, geotechnical conditions, drainage, water availability, power

supply, access roads, product handling, waste management and other site requirements.

Stage 5: Feasibility and Commercial Evaluation

Technical findings will need to be integrated with capital and operating cost estimates, environmental and social assessments, permitting requirements, logistics, market demand and financing considerations.

Stage 6: Implementation Preparation

Construction and procurement decisions should follow appropriate technical, commercial, regulatory and financing approvals.

The timing and completion of these stages will depend on the results of the project-development process.

## 4. ESG Integration Across the Project Lifecycle

ESG considerations are intended to form part of the project's development process, from early investigations through feasibility assessment, implementation and potential future operations.

During project development, the company expects to assess environmental and social risks, identify relevant mitigation measures, determine applicable approval requirements and establish appropriate monitoring arrangements.

As the project progresses, ESG priorities and performance indicators will be refined to reflect verified site conditions, regulatory obligations, operational requirements and stakeholder expectations.

## 5. Monitoring, Accountability and Continuous Improvement

GRAPHITE INDUSTRIES LIMITED intends to develop a practical approach to monitoring relevant environmental, social and governance performance as its organisational and project capabilities mature.

Potential indicators may include water use, energy consumption, waste management, workplace safety, employee training, gender representation, local procurement and stakeholder grievance resolution.

Targets and reporting arrangements will be established when suitable baselines, responsibilities and monitoring systems are available. The company will communicate ESG progress based on verified information and avoid presenting proposed commitments as completed achievements.

Our ESG Commitment

GRAPHITE INDUSTRIES LIMITED aims to pursue responsible graphite resource development by integrating environmental stewardship, inclusive social practices and accountable governance into its proposed mining and processing activities.

Our objective is to build a foundation for responsible industrial development, meaningful stakeholder relationships and long-term value creation in Tanzania.
